Our Program

How It Works

From cracked concrete to community art. Our pilot program turns one panel a month into a story the neighborhood tells together.

The Process

Four Steps, One Panel at a Time

01

Snap a Photo

Residents use our mobile app to photograph damaged sidewalk panels. The app automatically captures geolocation, so we know exactly where the hazard is. No forms. No phone trees. Just a photo and a tap.

Each submission is reviewed by our team to confirm it is a legitimate sidewalk panel (not a driveway or private walkway) and to assess whether it is a tripping hazard.

  • Photo upload with automatic GPS tagging
  • Simple category tags: crack, lift, root damage
  • Monthly submission window closes on the 15th
  • Anonymous or named submissions both accepted
02

Community Votes

Once a month, the neighborhood votes on which submitted panel matters most. The top-voted panel wins. This ensures we are fixing what the community actually cares about, not what an algorithm or a bureaucrat decides.

Voting is open to anyone in the neighborhood council district. One vote per verified address to prevent ballot stuffing.

  • Voting period: 7 days each month
  • Results announced publicly within 48 hours
  • Runner-up panels stay in the queue for next month
  • Property owner consent confirmed before work begins
03

Repair & Create

A licensed concrete crew removes the damaged panel, preps the base, and pours new concrete. Then our teaching artist leads neighborhood kids in designing and placing a colored concrete artwork (the owner picks the theme: a flower, a sun, a pattern, a family story) on the fresh surface.

The color pigment is mixed right into the concrete, so the art sits flush with the sidewalk with a slip-resistant finish. The goal is a smooth, firm walking surface that works for everyone, including people using wheelchairs and strollers. The process takes roughly two weekends. The first weekend is design: kids sketch the pattern and choose colors. The second is build: the crew places the colored concrete under the youth's direction, and kids help finish the surface.

  • Licensed, insured concrete crew handles all structural work
  • Teaching artist leads all youth design sessions
  • Integral color mixed into the concrete: flush and slip-resistant
  • No tiles, no grout, no raised edges: art you can walk on
  • City right-of-way permit obtained before any work begins
04

Celebrate & Share

We document every panel with professional before and after photography. The young artists get credit by name (or tag, if they prefer). Sponsors get public recognition on the panel and across our digital channels.

Then we move to the next panel. Same neighborhood. Same process. Same community. We start with Site 1 on Laurel in Beverly Grove and grow toward 10 panels in Year 1.

  • Professional photography and drone documentation
  • Social media campaign tagging artists and sponsors
  • Annual impact report for all funders
  • Panel added to permanent online gallery and map

The Future

Scaling Path

We are not building a single installation. We are building a repeatable model that works in any neighborhood with cracked sidewalks and kids who want to create.

Year 2: Scale to 30 panels across three neighborhoods. Revenue grows to approximately $95,000 through larger grants and expanded sponsorships. Total costs reach approximately $91,000. The program breaks even.

Year 3: Expand to 60 or more panels annually. By now, Sidewalk Stories LA has a track record, a portfolio of completed panels, and a diversified funding base. The program becomes self-sustaining.

Design Showcase

Colored Concrete, Art You Can Walk On

We repair the sidewalk properly, then place colorful integral-pigment concrete in whatever theme the owner chooses flush and slip-resistant. Durable, accessible by design, and beautiful.

Concept Render

A Flower in the Sidewalk

Neighborhood youth design the pattern, and the color is mixed into the concrete itself. The result sits flush with the walking surface: no trip hazards, no grout lines, no raised edges.

  • Flush surface with no elevation change, designed for accessibility
  • Broom / exposed-aggregate finish for slip resistance
  • Integral color fused into the concrete, so it won't peel or scrape off
  • Final treatment confirmed with the City before any work in the public right-of-way
Real Precedent

Proven in the Ground

Every panel becomes a landmark neighbors are proud of. Young artists get credit by name, and the community gets a safe, repaired sidewalk and a work of art in one.

Pictured: the Chinook Trails school sundial, a real colored concrete project by others. The flower above is a concept render; real projects like it (such as the colored cement flower at Castle Rock Hospital) show the method works.

Where we are headed: tile mosaics were the original dream behind Sidewalk Stories. Colored concrete gets us building now, and mosaics come next on partner plazas, walls and planters, then on the sidewalk once a tested, accessible panel is approved by the City.
