The Problem

LA Sidewalks Are Cracked, Dangerous, and Ignored

Thousands of miles of damaged sidewalks across Los Angeles create daily hazards for seniors, people with mobility challenges, and families with strollers. The repair backlog is measured in years, not months.

  • Seniors trip on lifted panels
  • Wheelchairs get stuck
  • Responsibility stuck between property owners and city
  • Damaged panels sit unrepaired for years
[Photo: Close-up of cracked, lifted sidewalk panel]
Our Solution

From Hazard to Public Art

A simple, community-driven process that turns broken sidewalk panels into lasting neighborhood treasures.

01

Snap a Photo

Residents photograph damaged sidewalk panels through our mobile app, automatically capturing location.

02

Community Votes

The neighborhood votes on which damaged panels matter most. The top-voted panel wins each month.

03

Repair & Create

A licensed crew fixes the panel. Then neighborhood kids and a teaching artist design and build a mosaic.

04

Celebrate & Share

We document the before and after, celebrate the young artists, and move to the next panel.

[Photo: Kids smiling and working on mosaic tiles with teaching artist]
Who Benefits

Three Groups Win Every Time

Pedestrians & Residents

Safer sidewalks, especially for seniors and people with limited mobility. A single repaired panel removes a tripping hazard that may have existed for years.

Youth Participants

Hands-on public art experience, civic pride, and a visible contribution to their neighborhood. Kids gain skills in design, teamwork, and public collaboration.

Local Businesses

Sponsored panels carry their logo and social visibility. Permanent public recognition on a mosaic panel, professional photography, and social media content.

Year One

The Pilot Program

Starting in the Fairfax/Mid-City West neighborhood, near Blackburn and Melrose.

10 Panels

Completed across 10 months in our pilot neighborhood, each one a small public art piece where a hazard used to be.

$38,700

Total pilot budget. $2,700 per panel covers concrete repair, mosaic materials, teaching artist stipend, and city permit.

100 Youth

Neighborhood kids engaged in public art creation, building civic pride and hands-on creative skills.
