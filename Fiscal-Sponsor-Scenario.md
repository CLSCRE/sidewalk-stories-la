# Sidewalk Stories LA — Fiscal Sponsor Scenario Analysis

_Comparing standalone 501(c)(3) formation vs. operating under an established LA nonprofit fiscal sponsor for Year 1._

---

## What Is a Fiscal Sponsor?

A fiscal sponsor is an existing 501(c)(3) nonprofit that agrees to house your project under its tax-exempt umbrella. Donations and grants are made to the sponsor, which then releases funds to your project (usually minus an administrative fee of 5% to 10%). The sponsor handles tax filings, bookkeeping, insurance, and compliance.

**Why it matters for Sidewalk Stories LA:**
- Most grants over $1,000 require 501(c)(3) status.
- Forming a standalone 501(c)(3) costs roughly $1,500 and takes 3 to 6 months.
- A fiscal sponsor lets you accept tax-deductible donations and apply for grants immediately.

## Year 1 Cost Comparison: Standalone vs. Fiscal Sponsor

| Cost Category | Standalone | Fiscal Sponsor | Savings |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 501(c)(3) formation + legal | $1,000 | $0 | **$1,000** |
| Bookkeeping / nonprofit admin | $3,600 ($300/mo) | $1,200 ($100/mo) | **$2,400** |
| General liability + program insurance | $3,000 ($250/mo) | $1,200 ($100/mo, or covered) | **$1,800** |
| Fiscal sponsor fee (7% of revenue) | $0 | ~$1,770 | **-$1,770** |
| App hosting & maintenance | $2,400 | $2,400 | $0 |
| Marketing & social | $1,200 | $1,200 | $0 |
| **Total Year 1 overhead + startup** | **$11,700** | **$7,770** | **$3,930** |
| Panel costs (10 panels) | $27,000 | $27,000 | $0 |
| **Total Year 1 budget** | **$38,700** | **$34,770** | **$3,930** |

**Bottom line:** A fiscal sponsor reduces Year 1 cash needs by roughly $3,900 (about 10% of the total budget). The founder bridge drops from $13,450 to approximately $9,520.

---

## Non-Financial Tradeoffs

| Factor | Standalone 501(c)(3) | Fiscal Sponsor |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed to grant-ready** | 3 to 6 months | Immediate |
| **Control over funds** | Full | Shared; sponsor must approve major expenses |
| **Branding** | Independent entity | Project of sponsor (can still brand as Sidewalk Stories LA) |
| **Board governance** | You build it | Sponsor's board has oversight |
| **Donor perception** | Direct tax deduction to your org | Donation goes to sponsor; donor may not know your project |
| **Long-term scalability** | Best for multi-year growth | Usually capped at 1-3 years |
| **Exit path** | Not applicable | Spin out into standalone 501(c)(3) later |

**Verdict:** For a 10-panel pilot with a $38K budget, a fiscal sponsor is almost certainly the right Year 1 choice. It accelerates grant eligibility, reduces overhead, and lets you test the model before committing to the full compliance burden of a standalone nonprofit.

## Recommended LA Fiscal Sponsor Options

### 1. Community Partners (Los Angeles)
- **Website:** communitypartners.org
- **Focus:** General fiscal sponsorship for LA-based social impact projects
- **Fee:** Typically 7% to 9% of revenue
- **Why fit:** They are the largest fiscal sponsor in LA and specialize in early-stage projects. They offer back-office support, HR, insurance, and grant administration.
- **Contact:** partnerships@communitypartners.org
- **Next step:** Submit the online inquiry form and schedule a 30-minute intake call.

### 2. Arts for LA
- **Website:** artsforla.org
- **Focus:** Arts advocacy and education in Los Angeles
- **Fee:** Typically 5% to 8%
- **Why fit:** Directly aligned with public art and youth arts education. Their network includes LA County Arts Commission and City of LA DCA contacts.
- **Contact:** info@artsforla.org
- **Next step:** Email a brief project description and ask if they accept new fiscally sponsored projects.

### 3. Social and Environmental Entrepreneurs (SEE) — Los Angeles
- **Website:** see-leadership.org
- **Focus:** Social entrepreneurs and early-stage nonprofits
- **Fee:** Typically 5% to 10%
- **Why fit:** SEE specializes in helping founders test ideas before formalizing a 501(c)(3). They offer coaching, network access, and fiscal sponsorship.
- **Contact:** info@see-leadership.org
- **Next step:** Review their "Fiscal Sponsorship" page and submit an inquiry.

### 4. Youth Policy Institute (YPI) — Los Angeles
- **Website:** ypiusa.org
- **Focus:** Youth development, education, and community programs in LA
- **Fee:** Typically 5% to 8%
- **Why fit:** YPI runs youth programs across LA and has deep experience with city contracts, youth insurance, and teaching artist networks.
- **Contact:** development@ypiusa.org
- **Next step:** Email a brief LOI asking about fiscal sponsorship for a youth public art pilot.

### 5. The Center for Cultural Innovation (CCI)
- **Website:** cci2.org
- **Focus:** Individual artists and small arts organizations in California
- **Fee:** Typically 5% to 7%
- **Why fit:** CCI is artist-focused and understands public art projects. They may be smaller and more flexible than Community Partners.
- **Contact:** info@cci2.org
- **Next step:** Submit an inquiry through their website.

## Recommended Approach

**Phase 1 (This month):** Reach out to Community Partners and Arts for LA in parallel. Both are strong fits but serve slightly different roles:
- Community Partners is the safest bet for back-office infrastructure.
- Arts for LA is the best bet for arts-specific credibility and grant relationships.

**Phase 2 (If both decline):** Approach YPI and SEE as backups.

**Phase 3 (Month 6-9):** If the pilot succeeds and revenue diversifies, begin planning a standalone 501(c)(3) spinout in Year 2. The fiscal sponsor agreement should explicitly allow this.

---

## Questions to Ask a Prospective Fiscal Sponsor

1. What is your administrative fee structure? Is it a percentage of revenue, a flat monthly fee, or both?
2. Do you provide general liability and youth program insurance, or do we need our own policy?
3. Can we maintain independent branding (Sidewalk Stories LA) while being fiscally sponsored?
4. What is your process for approving expenses and releasing funds?
5. Do you require your board to approve our project budget annually?
6. Can we spin out into a standalone 501(c)(3) later? What is the process and timeline?
7. Do you offer grant-writing support or introductions to funders?
8. What is the minimum or maximum project budget you accept?
9. How quickly do you disburse funds after a grant or donation is received?
10. Can we open a dedicated project bank account under your EIN?

## Decision Framework

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| If Community Partners accepts the project | **Go with Community Partners.** They are the gold standard for LA fiscal sponsorship. |
| If Arts for LA accepts but Community Partners declines | **Go with Arts for LA.** The arts-specific network is worth the slightly smaller back-office. |
| If both decline | **Form a standalone nonprofit immediately.** The delay in grant eligibility is less damaging than having no sponsor at all. |
| If both accept | **Interview both.** Compare fee structures, insurance coverage, and fund release timelines. Choose the one that feels like a partner, not a vendor. |
