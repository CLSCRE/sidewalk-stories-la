# Sidewalk Stories LA — Fiscal Sponsor Research List

_If Sidewalk Stories LA operates under a fiscal sponsor for Year 1, the sponsor provides its 501(c)(3) status, back office (bookkeeping, insurance, grant management), and credibility while the program builds a track record. In exchange, the sponsor charges an administrative fee, typically 5% to 15% of funds raised._

---

## What a fiscal sponsor provides

- Immediate 501(c)(3) status for grant applications
- General liability and D&O insurance coverage
- Bookkeeping, accounting, and audit infrastructure
- Payroll and contractor payment processing
- Donation processing and tax receipting
- Grant compliance and reporting support

## What it costs

- Administrative fee: 5% to 15% of all revenue passing through the sponsor
- Some sponsors charge a monthly base fee ($200 to $500) plus a percentage
- Sidewalk Stories LA would maintain its own branding and program identity

---

## Recommended Fiscal Sponsors in Greater Los Angeles

### 1. Community Partners (Los Angeles)
- **Website:** communitypartners.org
- **Focus:** Wide range of community based projects: arts, education, health, civic engagement
- **Fee:** 10% to 15% depending on services
- **Why fit:** Community Partners is the largest and most established fiscal sponsor in LA. They accept projects with clear community benefit and have supported dozens of arts and youth programs.
- **Contact:** General inquiry form on website; they respond within a few business days
- **Next step:** Submit an online inquiry describing the program, budget, and timeline

### 2. Social and Environmental Entrepreneurs (SEE) / Tides Center
- **Website:** tides.org
- **Focus:** Social justice, environmental, arts, and civic innovation projects
- **Fee:** 9% to 12%
- **Why fit:** Tides has a strong track record with civic and arts startups. They are more selective but offer strong back office support.
- **Contact:** tides.org/contact
- **Next step:** Review their project eligibility criteria online; submit a letter of inquiry

### 3. Arts for LA
- **Website:** artsforla.org
- **Focus:** Arts education and public arts projects specifically in Los Angeles
- **Fee:** Estimated 8% to 12%
- **Why fit:** Arts for LA exists to support LA based arts nonprofits and projects. A sidewalk mosaic program is directly in their wheelhouse.
- **Contact:** info@artsforla.org
- **Next step:** Email with a brief program description and ask if they currently sponsor similar projects

### 4. InnerCity Struggle / Building Healthy Communities
- **Website:** innercitystruggle.org
- **Focus:** Youth development, civic engagement, and neighborhood improvement in East LA
- **Fee:** Inquire (community based, may be lower)
- **Why fit:** If the pilot neighborhood is on the Eastside, InnerCity Struggle has deep community roots and youth networks.
- **Contact:** info@innercitystruggle.org
- **Next step:** Call and ask about fiscal sponsorship for a youth arts and public safety project

### 5. Community Corporation of Santa Monica (CCSM)
- **Website:** ccsm.org
- **Focus:** Affordable housing, community improvement, and youth programs on the Westside
- **Fee:** Inquire
- **Why fit:** If the pilot is on the Westside, CCSM has neighborhood credibility and infrastructure.
- **Contact:** General contact form

### 6. Youth Policy Institute (YPI) / Legacy Youth Workforce
- **Website:** ypiusa.org
- **Focus:** Youth education, workforce development, and community programs across LA
- **Fee:** Inquire
- **Why fit:** YPI is one of the largest youth serving nonprofits in LA. They have the infrastructure to absorb a small program easily.
- **Contact:** General contact form or info@ypiusa.org
- **Next step:** Email with a concise program summary and budget

### 7. Public Works / Community Arts Resources
- **Website:** Search for "LA public arts fiscal sponsor" or contact local arts councils
- **Focus:** Public art, community arts, creative placemaking
- **Fee:** Varies
- **Why fit:** Public art specific sponsors understand the permit, liability, and community engagement requirements better than general sponsors.
- **Next step:** Ask LA County Arts Commission or City of LA DCA for referrals to fiscal sponsors they have worked with

### 8. Local Community Foundation (as fiscal sponsor)
- **California Community Foundation (CCF)** sometimes sponsors small projects
- **Jewish Community Foundation of LA** if there is alignment with Jewish youth engagement
- **Asian Pacific Community Fund** or **California Latinx Community Foundation** if the pilot neighborhood aligns demographically

## Questions to Ask Each Fiscal Sponsor

1. What is your administrative fee structure (percentage, monthly minimum, setup fee)?
2. Do you provide general liability insurance that covers youth program activities and public right of way work?
3. Can we maintain our own branding, website, and social media, or must we operate under your name?
4. How quickly can you process grant applications and donor receipts?
5. Do you require a minimum annual budget or revenue?
6. What is the contract term, and what is the process for spinning off into our own 501(c)(3)?
7. Can you provide references from other sponsored projects?
8. Do you offer bookkeeping, payroll, and tax filing, or only donation processing?

---

## Recommendation

**Top choice: Community Partners.**
They are the most experienced, have the broadest acceptance criteria, and have supported dozens of LA arts and youth projects through their startup phase. The 10% to 15% fee is standard for the infrastructure and credibility they provide.

**Second choice: Arts for LA.**
If Arts for LA is willing and has capacity, their arts specific expertise makes grant applications and program reporting much easier.

**Decision point:** If you choose a fiscal sponsor, Year 1 fixed costs drop to near zero (no entity formation, no insurance, no bookkeeping software). The only costs are variable panel costs ($2,700 each), teaching artist stipends, and the sponsor's administrative fee. This makes the pilot extremely lean and fundable.

**Trade off:** You give up some autonomy and pay a percentage of every dollar raised. You also delay building your own 501(c)(3) brand equity. For a one year pilot, this is usually worth it.
