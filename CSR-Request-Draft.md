# Customer Service Request (CSR) Draft — Central District, Public Art on Sidewalk Panel

_For submission at dscsr.lacity.org once Trevor has created/logged into an Angeleno account. Paste the text below into the "New Request" description field, adjusting the category/department dropdowns to Central District / Bureau of Engineering as the site prompts._

---

**Category/Type:** Public art installation in public right-of-way (sidewalk panel), not a standard ADA sidewalk repair

**Location:** Fairfax / Mid-City West neighborhood, near Blackburn Ave and Melrose Ave, Los Angeles, CA 90048 (specific panel address to be confirmed once a pilot panel is selected)

**Request description:**

I am the founder of Sidewalk Stories LA, a California nonprofit public benefit corporation. We repair damaged public sidewalk panels and transform them into community-built mosaic art, designed and installed by neighborhood youth working with a professional teaching artist alongside a licensed concrete crew.

I am submitting this request on the guidance of Safe Sidewalks LA (sidewalks@lacity.org), who confirmed on 2026-07-22 that this type of mosaic art installation does not qualify under a regular A Permit issued through the Sidewalk Rebate Program, and advised that the art installation itself needs to be handled directly through Central District.

I would like guidance on:

1. What permit or approval process applies to installing mosaic art on a public sidewalk panel in the public right-of-way, once the underlying concrete repair is complete.
2. Whether this requires Cultural Affairs Commission review (per the Department of Cultural Affairs, who indicated it most likely does since this is public right-of-way, and that a free voluntary early review is available).
3. Typical timeline and any associated fees for this type of approval.
4. Any design, material, or safety requirements (for example, anti-slip surface requirements) specific to sidewalk-level public art.

Project details for reference:
- Surface area: a typical LA sidewalk panel, approximately 4 feet by 6 feet
- Materials: weather-resistant ceramic tile, grout, and sealant
- Structural work: a licensed concrete crew handles all concrete repair under a separate city permit before the mosaic is installed
- Youth participants: 6 to 10 neighborhood kids per panel, supervised by a teaching artist and nonprofit staff
- Pilot scope: 10 panels in Year 1, starting in the Fairfax / Mid-City West neighborhood

I am happy to provide additional documentation (nonprofit formation documents, insurance information, program plan) as needed. Thank you for your guidance.
