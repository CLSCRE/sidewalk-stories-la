## About Section

I am the founder of Sidewalk Stories LA, a California nonprofit public benefit corporation that repairs broken sidewalk panels and transforms them into community-built mosaic art designed and installed by neighborhood youth.

I live in the Fairfax / Mid-City West neighborhood of Los Angeles, where I walk past cracked and lifted sidewalk panels every day. I started Sidewalk Stories LA because I believe a single repaired panel can do two things at once: remove a hazard that has tripped seniors and snagged wheelchairs for years, and give neighborhood kids a chance to design and build something beautiful on the street where they live.

Our pilot program will complete 10 panels in Year 1, engaging roughly 60 to 100 local youth in public art creation. Each panel costs $2,700 all in: concrete repair, mosaic materials, teaching artist stipend, and city permit. The program is designed to be repeatable, neighborhood by neighborhood, until every cracked sidewalk in LA is either fixed or turned into a story.

Before Sidewalk Stories LA, I spent a decade in structured finance and nonprofit formation. That background translates directly to the work of building a credible, fundable, and scalable nonprofit from the ground up.

I am also a member of The Pottery Room in Culver City and a regular attendee at Urban Land Institute meetings, where I track LA infrastructure and pedestrian safety trends.

I am currently seeking grant partners, corporate sponsors, a founding tech partner, and a lead teaching artist for our pilot. If you care about public safety, youth arts, or civic infrastructure in Los Angeles, I would love to connect.

Learn more at sidewalkstoriesla.org.

**Post 2: The Problem**

LA has thousands of miles of damaged sidewalks.

The repair backlog is measured in years. Property owners are technically responsible but many cannot afford the $1,500 repair. The city has a backlog measured in decades.

So the cracks stay. And every day, seniors trip. Wheelchairs get stuck. Parents with strollers navigate obstacle courses.

Sidewalk Stories LA does not wait for bureaucracy. We fix the panel, and we turn the repair into a public art and youth education moment.

One panel at a time.

---

**Post 3: The Model**

How Sidewalk Stories LA works:

1. Resident snaps a photo of a cracked panel through our app
2. The neighborhood votes on which one matters most
3. A licensed crew repairs the concrete
4. Neighborhood kids design and build a mosaic on the fresh surface
5. We document, celebrate, and move to the next panel

Community driven. Youth led. Visually compelling. Fundable.

Pilot starts this fall.
