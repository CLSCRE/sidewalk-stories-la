# Sidewalk Stories LA — 12-Month Cash Flow Projection

_Year 1: July 2026 through June 2027. Based on the 10-panel pilot in Fairfax / Mid-City West._

---

## Assumptions

**Expense timing:**
- Startup costs hit in Month 1.
- Fixed operating costs ($850/mo) begin Month 1.
- Panels are completed at a rate of one per month for 10 months (Months 1-10), then pause in Months 11-12 for documentation and reporting.
- Each panel costs $2,700 and is paid in the month it is completed.

**Revenue timing:**
- Individual donations ramp from $250 to $750 per month.
- Corporate sponsors close as panels are confirmed (1 sponsor per panel starting Month 5).
- Small community arts grant ($5,000) arrives in Month 4 (Quarter 2).
- Medium youth development grant ($10,000) arrives in Month 10 (Quarter 4).
- Events / merchandise ($500) in Month 12.

## Key Cash Flow Insights

### 1. The Crunch Window: Months 7-9
The lowest point is Month 9 (March 2027) at approximately -$18,150 cumulative. This is the moment the bank account is most stressed. To avoid this:
- **Accelerate the medium grant.** If the $10,000 youth development grant can arrive in Month 7 or 8 instead of Month 10, the crunch is cut by roughly $10,000.
- **Front-load sponsors.** If 2-3 sponsors close in Months 1-3 instead of Months 5-9, the early burn is reduced by $1,500 to $2,250.
- **Increase early donations.** A small crowdfunding push in Month 1 (e.g., $2,000 from friends and family) takes the edge off the initial startup hit.

### 2. Grant Timing Is Everything
The small grant ($5,000) arriving in Month 4 provides a brief positive month, but the medium grant ($10,000) does not arrive until Month 10. By then, the cumulative deficit is already deep. For a funder reviewing this model, the key question is: "What happens if the $10,000 grant is delayed by 60 days?"

**Sensitivity analysis:** If the $10,000 grant shifts from Month 10 to Month 12, the lowest balance becomes approximately -$20,150. If it does not arrive at all, the gap is -$23,450.

### 3. The Minimum Founder Bridge
To keep the account above zero at all times, Trevor needs approximately **$18,500** in bridge funding available by Month 9. This is slightly higher than the $13,450 net gap because cash flows unevenly.

**Recommended bridge structure:**
- Trevor commits $10,000 as a founder loan or donation.
- Trevor secures a personal guarantor or line of credit for the remaining $8,500 as a backstop.
- As grants and sponsors arrive, the bridge is repaid or converted to equity equivalent (in nonprofit terms: a permanently restricted net asset contribution).

### 4. Sponsor Dependency
Five sponsors at $750 each ($3,750 total) are modeled to arrive in Months 5-9. If only 3 sponsors close, the gap widens by $1,500. If 8 sponsors close, the gap narrows by $2,250 and the bridge requirement drops to roughly $16,250.

### 5. What a Fiscal Sponsor Does to This Model
If Sidewalk Stories LA operates under a fiscal sponsor for Year 1:
- 501(c)(3) formation cost ($1,000) is deferred.
- Bookkeeping and nonprofit admin ($300/mo) is absorbed by the fiscal sponsor (or reduced to $100/mo).
- General liability insurance may be covered under the fiscal sponsor's policy (or reduced to $100/mo).
- **Estimated Year 1 savings:** $4,600 to $6,600.
- **New net gap:** approximately $7,000 to $9,000.
- **New bridge requirement:** approximately $12,000 to $14,000.

See the fiscal sponsor scenario document for a full comparison.

## Recommendations

1. **Secure the $10,000 grant application by Month 6.** Do not wait until Quarter 4 to apply. Most medium grants have 60-90 day review cycles. Apply in Month 6 to receive in Month 8.
2. **Close at least 2 sponsors in Months 1-3.** Early sponsor revenue changes the entire shape of the cash flow.
3. **Consider a fiscal sponsor for Year 1.** The overhead savings alone reduce the bridge requirement by roughly $5,000.
4. **Maintain a 90-day operating reserve rule.** After the medium grant arrives in Month 10, set aside $2,550 (3 months of fixed costs) before allocating any surplus to Year 2 scaling.
