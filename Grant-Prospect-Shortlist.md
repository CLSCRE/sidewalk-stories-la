# Sidewalk Stories LA — Grant Prospect Shortlist

_15 to 20 prospective funders aligned with youth arts, public safety, community improvement, and LA civic engagement. This is a starting list for Trevor to research, qualify, and prioritize._

---

## Tier 1: Local LA / Southern California Foundations (Highest Probability)

These funders have a geographic focus on Los Angeles and fund programs that combine youth development, arts, and community improvement.

### 1. The California Community Foundation (CCF)
- **Focus:** LA County arts, education, civic engagement
- **Typical award:** $5,000 to $50,000 for small nonprofits
- **Why fit:** CCF has specific initiatives around neighborhood improvement and youth. A mosaic sidewalk program is visually compelling and community-anchored.
- **Next step:** Review CCF's responsive grant calendar; many are open year-round with quarterly deadlines.

### 2. The Los Angeles County Arts Commission (now Dept. of Arts and Culture)
- **Focus:** Public art, community arts, youth arts education
- **Typical award:** $5,000 to $25,000 for small organizations
- **Why fit:** Directly funds public art in neighborhoods. Sidewalk mosaic is a natural fit.
- **Next step:** Check the Community Arts and Creative Placekeeping grant cycles.

### 3. The City of Los Angeles Department of Cultural Affairs (DCA)
- **Focus:** Public art, neighborhood arts, youth programs
- **Typical award:** $5,000 to $20,000
- **Why fit:** DCA funds both individual artists and organizations for public art in the right of way.
- **Next step:** Look at the Cultural Development and Public Art categories.

### 4. The Weingart Foundation
- **Focus:** Youth, education, community improvement in Southern California
- **Typical award:** $10,000 to $75,000 for small nonprofits
- **Why fit:** Explicitly funds programs that improve neighborhood conditions for youth.
- **Next step:** Confirm they accept unsolicited inquiries (they often do for small grants).

### 5. The Ralph M. Parsons Foundation
- **Focus:** Arts education, youth development, community services in LA County
- **Typical award:** $10,000 to $50,000
- **Why fit:** Parsons is a major LA arts and education funder with a long track record.
- **Next step:** Review their guidelines; they prefer established nonprofits but fund startups with strong plans.

### 6. The Rose Hills Foundation
- **Focus:** Youth, education, community programs in the San Gabriel Valley and broader LA
- **Typical award:** $5,000 to $25,000
- **Why fit:** Community improvement with youth involvement aligns well.

### 7. The Annenberg Foundation
- **Focus:** Arts, education, youth, community in LA
- **Typical award:** Wide range; larger grants often require relationship
- **Why fit:** Annenberg is a marquee LA funder. Hard to reach cold, but worth building toward.
- **Next step:** Start with their smaller responsive grant program, not the major initiative grants.

## Tier 2: Youth and Arts National Organizations (Good Fit, Competitive)

### 8. National Endowment for the Arts (NEA)
- **Focus:** Public art, community arts engagement
- **Typical award:** $10,000 to $100,000
- **Why fit:** NEA funds public art and community design projects. Sidewalk mosaic fits the "Our Town" or "Challenge America" categories.
- **Caveat:** Requires 501(c)(3) status or a fiscal sponsor. Cannot apply as an unincorporated group.

### 9. Americans for the Arts (AFTA) / Animating Democracy
- **Focus:** Civic engagement through arts
- **Typical award:** Varies; often technical assistance + small grants
- **Why fit:** The intersection of public art and civic repair is exactly their wheelhouse.

### 10. The National Guild for Community Arts Education
- **Focus:** Youth arts education in community settings
- **Typical award:** $5,000 to $25,000
- **Why fit:** Teaching artist + youth + public outcome is a strong match.

### 11. The Surdna Foundation
- **Focus:** Creative placemaking, youth development
- **Typical award:** $25,000 to $150,000
- **Why fit:** Surdna explicitly funds arts organizations that engage youth in shaping their built environment.
- **Caveat:** Competitive; strong track record helps.

### 12. The Robert R. McCormick Foundation
- **Focus:** Civic engagement, youth, community improvement
- **Typical award:** $10,000 to $50,000
- **Why fit:** McCormick funds programs that connect youth to civic life.

## Tier 3: Civic / Public Safety / Neighborhood Improvement

### 13. AARP Community Challenge
- **Focus:** Quick-action projects that improve livability for all ages
- **Typical award:** $2,500 to $15,000
- **Why fit:** Sidewalk repair directly improves pedestrian safety for seniors. Small, fast grant.
- **Next step:** Annual cycle, usually opens early in the year.

### 14. KaBOOM! / Play Everywhere Challenge
- **Focus:** Playful, community-driven public space improvements
- **Typical award:** $5,000 to $20,000
- **Why fit:** Turning a hazard into art is playful and community-driven.

### 15. The Awesome Foundation (LA Chapter)
- **Focus:** Small, awesome, community projects
- **Typical award:** $1,000 (no strings attached)
- **Why fit:** A $1,000 Awesome Foundation grant could fund the first mosaic materials for a pilot panel. Great for early momentum.
- **Next step:** Apply monthly; no nonprofit status required.

### 16. Patagonia Environmental Grants (if framed around walkability and urban greening)
- **Focus:** Environmental justice, sustainable communities
- **Typical award:** $5,000 to $20,000
- **Why fit:** If framed as reducing car dependency by making sidewalks walkable, it could qualify.
- **Caveat:** Stretch fit; only pursue if the environmental angle is genuine.

### 17. Safe Sidewalks LA (City of LA Rebate Program)
- **Focus:** Reimburses homeowners up to $10,000 for sidewalk repair
- **Why fit:** This is NOT a grant to the nonprofit, but it is a massive cost offset. If the nonprofit partners with a homeowner, the homeowner applies for the rebate and the nonprofit does the mosaic work.
- **Next step:** Confirm program rules and whether the rebate can be applied to a nonprofit-managed project.

## Tier 4: Corporate and Local Business Sponsorships

These are not grants but sponsorships, often easier to close in Year 1.

### 18. Local Business Sponsorships ($750 per panel)
- **Target:** Neighborhood restaurants, coffee shops, real estate agents, contractors
- **Pitch:** "Sponsor a mosaic panel for $750. Your logo goes on the mosaic. We tag you in all social content. You get a permanent public art piece in the neighborhood you serve."
- **Deliverable:** Before/after photos, social media content, press release mention, plaque on the panel.

### 19. Jewish Federation of Greater Los Angeles
- **Focus:** Jewish community programs, youth engagement, tikkun olam (repairing the world)
- **Typical award:** $5,000 to $25,000
- **Why fit:** Trevor referenced Jewish community programs as a youth channel. If Jewish youth participate, the Federation is a natural funder.

### 20. Neighborhood Council Grants (City of LA)
- **Focus:** Local improvement projects within specific council boundaries
- **Typical award:** $500 to $5,000
- **Why fit:** Each LA neighborhood council has a small discretionary budget. A single panel in their district is an easy local win.
- **Next step:** Identify the neighborhood council for the pilot area and attend a meeting.

## What to do next

1. **Confirm 501(c)(3) status or fiscal sponsor** (required for most grants above $1,000).
2. **Pick the pilot neighborhood** and identify its Neighborhood Council.
3. **Draft a one-page prospectus** (mission, problem, program, budget, outcomes) to send to Tier 1 funders.
4. **Build a grant calendar** with deadlines and requirements for each Tier 1 and Tier 2 funder.
5. **Approach 3 local businesses** in the pilot neighborhood with the sponsorship pitch.
