# Sidewalk Stories LA — City Permitting Contact Script

_Phone and email scripts for contacting the City of Los Angeles about sidewalk repair permits and Safe Sidewalks LA rebate rules._

---

## Who to Contact

### Primary: Safe Sidewalks LA Program (LADOT)
- **Department:** Los Angeles Department of Transportation (LADOT), Safe Sidewalks LA
- **Phone:** 311 (from LA) or (213) 473-3231
- **Website:** ladot.lacity.org/safesidewalks
- **Email:** safesidewalks@lacity.org (if available; confirm on website)
- **What they handle:** The Safe Sidewalks LA rebate program, which reimburses homeowners up to $10,000 for sidewalk repair adjacent to their property

### Secondary: Bureau of Street Services (StreetsLA)
- **Department:** StreetsLA (formerly Bureau of Street Services), City of Los Angeles
- **Phone:** (213) 847-6000
- **Website:** streetsla.lacity.org
- **What they handle:** City owned sidewalk repair, right of way permits, and infrastructure questions

### Tertiary: Department of Cultural Affairs (DCA)
- **Department:** City of LA Department of Cultural Affairs
- **Phone:** (213) 202-5500
- **What they handle:** Public art permits and approvals for artwork on city property or in the public right of way

---

## Script 1: Safe Sidewalks LA Rebate Inquiry (Phone)

**Goal:** Understand whether the Safe Sidewalks LA rebate can be used in partnership with a nonprofit, or if only individual homeowners can apply.

**Intro:**

> Hi, my name is Trevor Damyan. I am the founder of Sidewalk Stories LA, a nonprofit startup that repairs damaged sidewalk panels and installs community built mosaic art. I am calling to understand how the Safe Sidewalks LA rebate program works and whether our organization can partner with homeowners to access it.

**Key questions:**

1. Can a nonprofit organization apply for the Safe Sidewalks LA rebate, or does the application have to come from the individual property owner?
2. If a homeowner applies for the rebate, can they hire or partner with a nonprofit (Sidewalk Stories LA) to manage the repair and mosaic installation?
3. What documentation does the homeowner need to submit, and what is the typical reimbursement timeline?
4. Are there restrictions on adding mosaic art or decorative elements to a repaired sidewalk panel under the rebate program?
5. If the rebate only covers the structural repair, can a nonprofit raise separate funds for the mosaic materials and teaching artist?
6. What is the current wait time for rebate approval, and are there any neighborhoods that are prioritized?
7. Are there any neighborhoods where the rebate is not available or where the program is backlogged?

**Closing:**

> Thank you for your time. Could you send me the program guidelines and application by email? My address is trevor@sidewalkstoriesla.org. Also, is there a specific contact person at LADOT or StreetsLA I should speak with about nonprofit partnerships?

## Script 2: Bureau of Street Services — Right of Way Permit (Phone or Email)

**Goal:** Confirm the permit required for a nonprofit to legally repair a sidewalk panel and install mosaic art on public right of way.

**Email draft:**

> **Subject:** Inquiry: Permit requirements for nonprofit sidewalk repair and public mosaic art installation
>
> Dear StreetsLA Permit Office,
>
> My name is Trevor Damyan, and I am the founder of Sidewalk Stories LA, a California nonprofit public benefit corporation. Our mission is to improve pedestrian safety and youth civic engagement by repairing damaged public sidewalks and installing community built mosaic art on the repaired panels.
>
> We are planning a pilot program in [neighborhood name] and would like to confirm the permit requirements for the following activities:
>
> 1. Removing and replacing a damaged concrete sidewalk panel on public right of way (city property or adjacent to a private property)
> 2. Installing a decorative mosaic surface on the repaired panel using tile, grout, and sealant
> 3. Working with a licensed concrete contractor and youth volunteers under adult supervision
>
> Specifically, we need guidance on:
> - Which permit or encroachment application applies to our work
> - Whether a licensed contractor must hold the permit, or if the nonprofit can hold it
> - Insurance and bonding requirements
> - Any design or material restrictions for artwork in the public right of way
> - Typical permit processing time and cost
> - Whether we need separate approval from the Department of Cultural Affairs for the mosaic artwork
>
> We are happy to schedule a brief call or visit your office to discuss the project in more detail. Our pilot budget is modest, and we want to ensure full compliance from day one.
>
> Thank you for your assistance.
>
> Trevor Damyan
> Founder, Sidewalk Stories LA
> Email: trevor@sidewalkstoriesla.org
> Phone: [your phone]

## Script 3: Department of Cultural Affairs — Public Art Approval (Phone or Email)

**Goal:** Confirm whether mosaic art on a sidewalk panel requires public art approval from DCA.

**Email draft:**

> **Subject:** Public art inquiry: Community mosaic on repaired sidewalk panel
>
> Dear DCA Public Art Team,
>
> I am writing on behalf of Sidewalk Stories LA, a nonprofit startup that repairs damaged sidewalk panels and transforms them into small community built mosaics designed by local youth and teaching artists.
>
> We are preparing a pilot project in a Los Angeles neighborhood and want to confirm whether the mosaic component of our work requires a public art permit or approval from the Department of Cultural Affairs. The mosaic would be installed flush with the sidewalk surface, using exterior grade tile, grout, and sealant, and would be maintained by the property owner or the nonprofit.
>
> Our questions are:
> 1. Does a surface level mosaic on a sidewalk panel in the public right of way qualify as public art under DCA jurisdiction?
> 2. If so, what is the application process, timeline, and cost?
> 3. Are there design review requirements or material restrictions?
> 4. Can youth participants (ages 10 to 17) be involved in the physical installation under adult supervision, or must all installation work be done by licensed professionals?
> 5. Is there a DCA grant program that could help fund community public art projects like this one?
>
> We would welcome the opportunity to present our concept to your team and receive guidance on how to proceed in full compliance with city requirements.
>
> Thank you for your time.
>
> Trevor Damyan
> Founder, Sidewalk Stories LA
> trevor@sidewalkstoriesla.org
> [phone]

## Script 4: Follow-Up Call After Initial Inquiry

**Goal:** Get a status update if you have not heard back within 5 business days.

> Hi, this is Trevor Damyan with Sidewalk Stories LA. I sent an email on [date] about permit requirements for our nonprofit sidewalk repair and mosaic program. I am following up to see if there is someone I can speak with about:
> - Safe Sidewalks LA rebate rules for nonprofit partnerships, and
> - Right of way permits for sidewalk panel repair and mosaic installation.
>
> We are trying to line up our pilot timeline and want to make sure we have the correct permits in place before we begin fundraising. Could you direct me to the right person or department?
>
> My email is trevor@sidewalkstoriesla.org and my phone is [number]. Thank you.

---

## Key Information to Have Ready Before Any Call

- **Entity status:** If formed, "Sidewalk Stories LA, Inc., a California nonprofit public benefit corporation." If not yet formed, say "a nonprofit in formation."
- **Pilot neighborhood:** Have a specific neighborhood or council district in mind.
- **Property owner:** If you already have a willing property owner for the first panel, mention it.
- **Contractor status:** "We will use a licensed, insured concrete contractor."
- **Insurance status:** "We carry general liability and youth program insurance." (Even if you do not yet, say you are obtaining it.)
- **Budget:** "Our pilot program budget is approximately $38,700 for 10 panels."

## Expected Timeline

| Step | Estimated Time |
|---|---|
| Initial phone/email inquiry | Week 1 |
| Follow-up if no response | Week 2 |
| Permit application submitted | Week 3 to 4 |
| Permit approval | 4 to 8 weeks |
| Safe Sidewalks rebate application (if applicable) | Concurrent with permit |
| **Total before first panel work** | **8 to 12 weeks** |

Plan accordingly. Do not promise funders or sponsors a panel completion date until the permit pathway is confirmed.
