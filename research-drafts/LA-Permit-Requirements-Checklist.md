# Sidewalk Stories LA: LA Permit Requirements Checklist

_Step-by-step checklist for repairing a sidewalk panel and installing a community mosaic in the public right of way in the City of Los Angeles. Based on public information from the Bureau of Engineering, StreetsLA, and the Department of Cultural Affairs. Current as of 2026-07-20. Confirm all fees and timelines directly with city offices before work begins._

---

## Key city departments and contacts

| Department | Role | Contact |
|---|---|---|
| **Bureau of Engineering (BOE)** | Issues Class A Construction Permits for sidewalk repair/replacement | sidewalks@lacity.org |
| **StreetsLA** | Public right-of-way maintenance, tree-related sidewalk damage, some permit intake | StreetsLA.ERT@lacity.org; 311 |
| **Department of Cultural Affairs (DCA)** | Public art review for artwork on city property or public right of way | dca.review@lacity.org; DCA.PublicArt@lacity.org |
| **MyLA311** | Service requests and rebate applications | myla311.lacity.org |
| **Mid-City West Neighborhood Council** | Local support letters and community improvement grants | outreach@midcitywest.org |

## Step-by-step checklist

### Phase 1: Project planning and site identification

- [ ] Identify a specific damaged sidewalk panel in the pilot neighborhood.
- [ ] Photograph the panel from multiple angles, including close-ups of cracks, lifts, and root damage.
- [ ] Confirm the panel is on public right of way (sidewalk), not a private driveway or walkway.
- [ ] Identify the adjacent property owner and obtain written consent for the repair and mosaic.
- [ ] Confirm whether the damage is tree-root-related (StreetsLA / BOE no-fee A-Permit may apply) or structural failure (standard BOE A-Permit).
- [ ] Verify that the panel location is within City of Los Angeles boundaries and the Mid-City West neighborhood.

### Phase 2: City engagement and preliminary approvals

- [ ] Email sidewalks@lacity.org with the project overview, location, and question list from City-Permitting-Contact-Script.md.
- [ ] Email dca.review@lacity.org to ask whether the mosaic component requires DCA public art review.
- [ ] Email outreach@midcitywest.org to introduce the project and request a community improvement grant application and a support letter.
- [ ] Attend one Mid-City West Neighborhood Council board meeting if required for grant eligibility.
- [ ] Document all city responses in a shared project folder.

### Phase 3: Permits for sidewalk repair

- [ ] Determine the correct BOE permit:
  - **Class A Construction Permit** for sidewalk repair, replacement, or new construction.
  - **No-Fee A-Permit** if damage is caused by street tree roots (photo documentation required).
- [ ] Confirm whether the nonprofit, the property owner, or the licensed contractor must hold the permit.
- [ ] Hire a licensed, insured concrete contractor (C-8 Concrete & Masonry license preferred).
- [ ] Request a certificate of insurance from the contractor naming Sidewalk Stories LA as additional insured.
- [ ] Prepare a site sketch with dimensions, adjacent improvements, and ADA compliance notes.
- [ ] Submit the A-Permit application to BOE, including:
  - Site plan / sketch
  - Contractor license and insurance
  - Scope of work (panel removal, base prep, pour, cure)
  - Property owner consent letter
  - Fee payment (unless no-fee tree root permit)
- [ ] Wait for BOE review and approval (estimated 4 to 8 weeks).

### Phase 4: Public art review (if required)

- [ ] If DCA confirms the mosaic qualifies as public art, submit a public art review application.
- [ ] Prepare design materials:
  - Mosaic design concept
  - Materials list (tile, grout, sealant)
  - Maintenance plan
  - Artist / teaching artist resume
  - Youth engagement plan
- [ ] Respond to DCA design review comments and obtain approval.
- [ ] Confirm whether the Cultural Affairs Commission review is required for the proposed mosaic.

### Phase 5: Safe Sidewalks LA rebate (optional but important)

- [ ] Confirm whether the property owner can apply for the Safe Sidewalks LA rebate and use Sidewalk Stories LA as the project manager.
- [ ] If permitted, help the property owner submit the rebate application through MyLA311.
- [ ] Note: the rebate program is currently on a waitlist due to funding constraints. New applications are placed in a queue.
- [ ] Clarify whether the rebate can cover only the structural concrete repair or also the mosaic materials and teaching artist.

### Phase 6: Right-of-way encroachment / art installation permits (if required)

- [ ] If the mosaic is considered a fixture, structure, or encroachment, determine whether an R-Permit (Revocable Permit) is required.
- [ ] Gather R-Permit requirements:
  - Sketch with dimensions of the sidewalk area
  - ADA compliance verification for adjacent sidewalks
  - Clearances from street trees, lights, utility poles, fire hydrants
  - Proof of liability insurance
  - Waiver of Damages recorded with the County
  - Approval by the applicable BOE District Engineer
- [ ] Submit R-Permit application if required.

### Phase 7: Insurance and liability

- [ ] Obtain general liability insurance covering public right-of-way work and youth programs.
- [ ] Confirm the policy names the property owner and the City of Los Angeles as additional insured if required.
- [ ] Obtain abuse/molestation liability coverage because minors participate.
- [ ] Verify the contractor’s workers’ compensation insurance is active.

### Phase 8: Youth participation and safety

- [ ] Confirm city rules on whether youth ages 10 to 17 may participate in the physical installation or only the design phase.
- [ ] If youth installation is permitted, ensure a licensed contractor or teaching artist supervises all hands-on work.
- [ ] Require closed-toe shoes, gloves, goggles, and age-appropriate tools for all youth participants.
- [ ] Obtain signed liability waivers and photo releases from parents/guardians.
- [ ] Confirm background checks for all adults working directly with youth.

### Phase 9: Construction and installation

- [ ] Post permitted work notices if required by BOE.
- [ ] Schedule the licensed concrete crew for panel removal, base prep, pour, and cure.
- [ ] Schedule the teaching artist and youth participants for design workshop(s) and mosaic installation.
- [ ] Ensure ADA compliance for slopes, cross-slopes, and surface finish.
- [ ] Use exterior-grade, slip-resistant, weather-resistant tile, grout, and sealant.
- [ ] Install the mosaic flush with the sidewalk surface to avoid tripping hazards.
- [ ] Photograph every stage for city inspection, sponsor deliverables, and grant reports.

### Phase 10: Inspection, completion, and maintenance

- [ ] Request BOE inspection of the concrete repair.
- [ ] Address any inspection corrections promptly.
- [ ] Install sponsor plaque if applicable.
- [ ] Document the completed panel with before/after photography and drone footage if available.
- [ ] Confirm maintenance responsibility:
  - Property owner typically maintains adjacent sidewalk under California Streets and Highway Code Section 5610.
  - Sidewalk Stories LA should have a written maintenance or monitoring agreement with the property owner or city if required.
- [ ] Submit any rebate reimbursement documentation to the property owner or Safe Sidewalks LA program.

## Estimated costs

| Item | Estimated Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| BOE Class A Permit fee | $200-$600 | Varies by scope; no-fee if tree-root damage |
| DCA public art review | $0-$500 | Confirm whether fee applies |
| R-Permit (if required) | $200-$1,000+ | Depends on encroachment complexity |
| Concrete repair | $900 per panel | Per program budget |
| Mosaic materials | $1,000 per panel | Tile, grout, sealant, safety gear |
| Teaching artist stipend | $400 per panel | Per program budget |
| General liability insurance | $2,000-$6,000/year | Depends on coverage limits |
| Additional insured endorsements | Included or small fee | Per contractor / policy |
| Safe Sidewalks LA rebate | Up to $12,400 per lot | Reimburses property owner; currently waitlisted |

---

## Insurance requirements summary

| Requirement | Minimum Suggested | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| General liability | $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate | Must cover public right-of-way work |
| Abuse/molestation liability | $1M per occurrence | Required because minors participate |
| Workers’ compensation | CA statutory | Required if any W-2 employees |
| Auto liability | $1M | If organization vehicles transport materials |
| Additional insured | Property owner, City of LA, contractor | Add as required by permit or contract |

## Estimated timeline

| Step | Estimated Time |
|---|---|
| Site identification and property owner consent | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Initial city email/phone inquiries | 1 week |
| BOE A-Permit application and approval | 4 to 8 weeks |
| DCA public art review (if required) | 4 to 12 weeks |
| Safe Sidewalks rebate application | Concurrent; waitlist dependent |
| Contractor scheduling and concrete work | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Mosaic design and installation workshops | 2 weekends |
| BOE inspection and final documentation | 1 to 2 weeks |
| **Total from site selection to finished panel** | **10 to 20 weeks** |

## Critical open questions to confirm with the city

1. Can a nonprofit hold the A-Permit, or must the property owner or contractor hold it?
2. Does a surface-level mosaic on a repaired sidewalk panel require DCA review?
3. Does the mosaic require an R-Permit because it is art in the right of way?
4. Can youth participants ages 10 to 17 physically install tile under adult supervision, or must installation be done only by licensed professionals?
5. Can the Safe Sidewalks LA rebate be used for a nonprofit-managed project where the property owner captures the rebate?
6. What are the current wait times for BOE A-Permit approval in the Fairfax / Mid-City West area?
7. Are there any design or material restrictions for public art on sidewalks (e.g., slip resistance, color, embedded objects)?
